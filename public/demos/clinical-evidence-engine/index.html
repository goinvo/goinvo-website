How Quality is Determined
Based on GRADE methodology · Guideline Central grading standards
Recommendation Strength
StrongBenefits clearly outweigh the risks — most patients in this situation should follow the recommendation
ConditionalBenefits and risks are closely balanced — patient values, preferences, and circumstances should guide the decision
Evidence Quality Grades
Grade AHigh quality — Consistent evidence from well-designed RCTs, systematic reviews, or meta-analyses
Grade BModerate quality — RCTs with some limitations, or strong consistent observational evidence
Grade CLow quality — Observational studies, case series, or RCTs with significant limitations
Grade DVery low quality — Expert opinion, clinical consensus, or evidence extrapolated from other populations
Source: GRADE Working Group · Guideline Central
